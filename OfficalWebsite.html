Our Foundation

Who We Are

Chi'ling Inc. is a nonprofit built on a simple truth: across the vast mosaic of AAPI cultures, our shared experiences far outnumber our differences. We are multiracial, multilingual, multigenerational — and we are one community.

Founded on the belief that young people deserve spaces to explore their identities without apology, we bring together youth from across counties, schools, and states to learn, advocate, and lead.

From Chinese school folk tales to modern-day civic engagement, we trace the full arc of what it means to be Asian American and Pacific Islander in the United States today.

What We Stand For

Three Pillars of Our Work

🌺

Cultural Identity

Reconnecting AAPI youth with their heritage — from the stories told at Chinese school to the living traditions that define who we are. We don't ask you to choose between cultures. We celebrate the whole of you.

🧠

Mental Health & Wellbeing

Confronting the model minority myth head-on. We provide spaces for honest conversation about mental health, intergenerational trauma, academic pressure, and what it costs to be unseen.

📢

Advocacy & Civic Voice

From OCA's AAPI curriculum legislation to local school board engagement, we train the next generation of advocates who understand that their story belongs in the public record.

Roots & Recognition

Our Heritage, Our Strength

The AAPI experience in America is not a footnote — it is a central chapter. We carry within us the memory of exclusion acts, internment, model minority weaponization, and pandemic-era hate. We also carry the memory of building railroads, laboratories, legislatures, and communities.

Chi'ling Inc. begins at the foundation — the stories told at Chinese school, the traditions passed down in kitchens and temples — and builds toward an understanding of what we bring to the table in contemporary American life.

Through our genome, our multiracial identity, and our shared human ancestry, we illuminate what science confirms: we are more alike than different. Fear of the other dissolves under the light of that truth.

A note on history: MOCO High School graduate Quintin Tai (Class of ~2004) contributes firsthand historical context on AAPI discrimination from 25 years ago. Contact: quillyt@gmail.com
